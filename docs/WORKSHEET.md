## Before you start

**Controls.** Arrow keys or WASD to walk · **Z** to talk and to examine · **X** to go back · **N** for your notebook.

**Fill this in as you go.** Each section matches a chapter of the game. Stop and write when you reach the end of a section — do not wait until the end, because you will not remember which of six people told you a thing, and *who told you* is half of what you are being asked for.

**Two rules for the whole sheet:**

1. **Answer in your own words.** One or two sentences is plenty. You are not being graded on length.
2. **Always name your source.** If a person told you, name the person. If you read it on a paper, name the paper. If you saw it yourself, write *saw it*.

**About the papers.** Every document in this game is labelled either **CLOSE** (it follows a real surviving document closely) or **RECONSTRUCTED** (written in the form and language of the record, but not copied from one specific surviving sheet). That label is on the reader screen. Write down which one you got — historians care about this, and so does this assignment.

## Part 8 — Ranking the causes · **16 points**

Historians have argued about this for three hundred years. Almost nobody thinks it was one thing. The game names four cases and the historian who makes each one:

| | The case | Who argues it |
|---|---|---|
| **A** | **LAND AND MONEY** — a village split into two factions, feuding families, a disputed boundary, and a pattern in who accused whom | Boyer & Nissenbaum, *Salem Possessed* (1974) |
| **B** | **THE WAR** — a brutal war to the north, and refugees from it living in the very households that did most of the accusing | Mary Beth Norton, *In the Devil's Snare* (2002) |
| **C** | **WHO WAS ACCUSED** — mostly women, and often women who stood to inherit property or had already been difficult in public | Carol Karlsen, *The Devil in the Shape of a Woman* (1987) |
