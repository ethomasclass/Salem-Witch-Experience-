# Walkthrough

Everything you need to do, in order, with where things are.

This exists for the teacher, not the student. A student should wander — the
goal tracker in the corner is enough for them. This is so you know what is in
there, can check a stuck class, and can find any single document quickly.

---

## The two systems, which are easy to confuse

**EXAMINE** — things you *see*. The boundary stone, the woodpile, the seating
chart, the account book, a bench, a threshold. Stand next to it, face it,
press **Z**, read the lines. It records itself. You will see *"Noted in your
notebook"* in the top-left. **There is nothing further to do with it.**

**READ** — the fifteen papers, and only the papers. Press **Z** once and a
full-screen reader opens: original text on the left, plain modern English on
the right, citation at the bottom. **Opening it copies it** — there is no
second key. Z or X closes it again.

**The game now tells you which is which.** Stand facing anything you can
interact with and a small yellow prompt appears over your head:

- **`Z · talk`** — a person
- **`Z · look`** — something you examine. One press and it is recorded.
- **`Z · read`** — a document. One press opens it and copies it.
- **`Z · read again`** — a document already in your notebook.

If a paper is still locked the prompt says **`Z · look`**, not `read` — and
pressing Z tells you exactly what to go and do first.

If no prompt appears, there is nothing in front of you. Turn, or step closer.

**Press N for the notebook, then ← or → to switch tabs:**
`What I was told` and `What I copied down  n/15`.

### Documents are locked until you have earned them

Four of the March papers will not open until you have examined the thing that
gives you a reason to read them. Try one early and you get a line like *"Papers
on the table, in more than one hand. They mean nothing to you yet."* That is
the game telling you to go and look at something first.

| Examine this first | …then this paper opens | …in this building |
|---|---|---|
| The parsonage **woodpile** | The 1689 village agreement | The parsonage |
| The meetinghouse **seating chart** | The seating list | The meetinghouse |
| Ingersoll's **account book** | The account book page | The tavern |
| The **boundary marker** in the woods | The Topsfield petition | **The Putnam house** |

That last row is the only genuinely obscure link in the game — the stone is
at the top of the map and the paper it unlocks is in a house at the bottom.

### The arrow at the edge of the screen

A small yellow chevron appears at the screen edge pointing toward whatever
the current goal is, with the distance in tiles. It **disappears the moment
the target is on screen** — if you can see the place, you do not need an
arrow.

If the goal is inside a building, it points at that building's door. If it is
further away than that — the jail, from the village — it points at the road
out.

It is deliberately not a minimap. A map in the corner makes players watch the
corner instead of the village, and noticing the village is the entire point.

### The goal box counts within a goal

`GOAL 2/6 · 2 of 4` means: second of six goals in this chapter, and two of the
four things in *this* goal are done. Goals that involve several things always
name them and count them, so a student can tell whether the last five minutes
counted for anything.

### Chapter exits are gated

You cannot leave a chapter until its goals are done. Walk onto the exit early
and the game steps you back and names the one thing still missing.

## Controls

| | |
|---|---|
| Move | Arrow keys or WASD |
| Talk, examine, advance, copy | **Z** (or Space / Enter) |
| Close a reader, back out of a conversation | **X** (or Esc) |
| Notebook | **N** — then ← → to switch tabs |

## Chapter 0 · The memorial, present day

You start on Charter Street, in the road, facing a low granite wall.

1. **Walk north** onto the pavement. A short arrival plays.
2. **Keep walking north through the gap in the wall.** You cross the threshold
   stones and they fire automatically — this is the inscriptions that stop
   mid-sentence. *(Too important to leave to chance, so it triggers on being
   walked over rather than examined.)*
3. **The three panels** are on the pavement outside the wall, to your left and
   right as you came in. Stand *below* each and face **up**:
   - *What happened* — leftmost
   - *The court* — next along
   - *Why?* — over to the right, past the entrance
   Panel three is the only place the game asks its question out loud, and it
   turns on the standing "What caused it?" line under the goal box.
4. **Rebecca Nurse's bench** — walk to the **top wall**, and it is the bench
   immediately **left of the gap**. Face up, press Z. This is the one that
   matters; you learn the date she dies before you ever meet her.
5. **Nora** is sitting on the wall on the **right-hand side**, near the
   entrance. Talk to her. Work through her topics — the shop question only
   appears if you have looked at the gift shop across the street first.
6. **Exit: the gap in the north wall**, right beside Rebecca Nurse's bench.

*Optional:* any of the other nineteen benches; the interpretive sign; the shop
window across the street (unlocks one of Nora's topics).

## Chapter 1 · March 1692

You arrive at the **south end of the road**, in Salem Town, between two large
houses. **Walk north.** It takes about thirty seconds and the arrival in the
village fires on its own.

Village layout, once you are there:

```
              boundary marker (woods, top-left)
                      |
    Nurse house       |        tavern
    (west)        MEETINGHOUSE   (east)
                      |
   ===================+=================  main road
                      |
    parsonage       well        Putnam house
    (south-west)              (south-east)
                      |
                 road south to Salem Town
```

### The four things to examine

1. **The woodpile** — right beside the parsonage, south-west. Stand below or
   above it, face it, Z.
2. **The seating chart** — go *inside* the meetinghouse (door in the middle of
   its front wall). The chart is on the wall by the door; stand below and face
   up.
3. **The boundary marker** — **north, in the woods, well left of centre.** A
   small knee-high stone where the trees thin out. Easy to miss. Approach from
   below or from the right.
4. **The account book** — inside the tavern, east. It is on the first table.

### The six people

- **Tituba** and **Rev. Parris** — inside the parsonage
- **Rebecca Nurse** — inside her house, west
- **Ann Putnam Jr.** — inside the Putnam house, south-east
- **Mercy Lewis** — outside, in the Putnam dooryard
- **Nathaniel Ingersoll** — inside the tavern

Work through every topic with each. Topics disappear once asked, so keep
going until only *"Say nothing more"* is left.

**The gossip link:** ask Ingersoll or Mercy *"What is the talk?"* first — that
gives you the witch cake, which unlocks a topic on Tituba that does not
otherwise exist.

### The four papers

| Paper | Where | Needs |
|---|---|---|
| The village agreement | Parsonage, on the table | woodpile |
| The seating list | Meetinghouse, right of the chart | seating chart |
| The account book page | Tavern, further down the table | account book |
| The Topsfield petition | **Putnam house**, on the table | **boundary marker** |

### Exit

**Behind the parsonage, just north of it**, the ground dips. Walk onto it.

## Interlude A · The parsonage cellar hole, present day

You land standing inside the excavated foundation.

1. **Examine the cellar floor** — you are standing on it. Face up or down and Z.
2. **Dr. Reyes** is in the cellar with you, on the right. Ask her everything —
   you need *"This is small"* and *"What was it like to live here?"* to move on.
3. *Optional:* the interpretive sign, on the grass south of the hole.
4. **Exit: south, out through the gap in the cellar wall**, then keep going
   south to the brick path.

---

## Chapter 2 · June 1692

The village again, three months later. Carts, strangers, dust.

1. **Francis Nurse** is standing in his dooryard, west, outside his house. Ask
   him where she is, about the petition, and why her.
2. **The petition** — go inside the Nurse house. It is on the table. She is
   not there.
3. **The warrant** — inside the tavern, on the far table.
4. **The deposition** — inside the meetinghouse, on the court table.
5. **Walk south down the road to Salem Town.** The **jail** is a low building
   on the **left-hand side**, about two-thirds of the way down, with barred
   windows. Go in.
6. **In the jail:** the bars run across the room. **Stand below the bars and
   press Z — interaction reaches through the grate.**
   - **Rebecca Nurse** is on the left.
   - **Tituba** is on the right.
   - **The jail keeper's account** is on the table on your side.
   - *Optional:* the straw on the floor.

   > **Rebecca Nurse's fourth topic is *"Tell her what happens on the
   > nineteenth of July."*** It only appears if you read her bench at the
   > memorial. This is the scene the whole game is built for. She does not
   > believe you.

7. **Back in the village:** talk to **Mary Warren** (tavern), **Ann Putnam
   Jr.** (Putnam house), and **Mercy Lewis** (outside, Putnam dooryard).
8. *Optional:* the two visitors on the road, the man drinking in the tavern.
9. **Exit: the north side of the meetinghouse.** Walk around behind it.

---

## Interlude B · The trial papers, present day

A reading room. **Dr. Whitfield** is on the right.

Ask her everything. You need four of the five:

- How could the court accept that evidence? *(spectral evidence)*
- Did they have lawyers?
- Who gave this court its authority? *(the lost charter)*
- Why did anyone confess?
- **Was it ergot poisoning?** — required. This is where the famous tidy
  explanation gets taken apart.

*Optional:* the boxes on the shelves, the sheet on the table.

**Exit: the door at the bottom of the room.**

---

## Chapter 3 · September 1692

You arrive on the road by the meetinghouse. The village is nearly empty.

1. **The neighbour** is mending a fence, east of centre, near the stone wall.
   He is the only one who will really talk. Ask him all three: Giles Corey,
   where they are buried, and whether it is over.
2. **Three papers in the meetinghouse** — on the court table: the Corey
   record, the death warrant return, and Mary Easty's petition.
3. **The sheriff's inventory** — nailed to a doorpost at the **Putnam house**,
   outside, on its south side.
4. **The Putnam house — go inside.** This is the noticing. Ann Putnam Jr. looks
   directly at you and asks whether you have been here before. **There is no
   dialogue option. You cannot answer.** It happens once.
5. *Optional but worth it:* the empty house in the far south-west corner;
   Francis Nurse; the woman at the well; Parris in the parsonage; Ingersoll in
   the empty tavern.
6. **Exit: the north side of the meetinghouse**, same as June.

---

## Interlude C · The memorial again

Same map as the opening. You now know every name on the wall.

1. **Ellen Towne-Putnam** is on a bench on the **left-hand side**. Ask her
   everything — you need the Ann Putnam topic specifically.
2. **The last three papers** are on the grass in the middle: Ann Putnam Jr.'s
   1706 confession, Sewall's 1697 apology, and the 2022 act.
3. *Optional:* Nora is still there.

When those are done **the closing screen opens by itself**: *What caused it?*

Type an answer. **Enter** to finish. Nothing is scored and the game does not
respond to the content.

4. **Press "Copy my notes"** below the game. That is the exit artifact — the
   answer, every piece of evidence with its source, and every document with
   its citation, as plain text.

## If a class gets stuck

- **"I can't leave."** The exit is gated. Walk onto it and the game names the
  one thing still missing. Or press N and compare against this list.
- **"The paper won't open."** It is knowledge-gated — see the table at the
  top. Almost always the boundary marker.
- **"I can't reach the people in the jail."** Stand directly below the bars
  and press Z. Interaction reaches through.
- **"I missed the Rebecca Nurse conversation option."** The warning topic
  requires having read her bench at the memorial in chapter zero.
- **Progress is saved automatically** to that browser only. "Start over"
  erases it.
